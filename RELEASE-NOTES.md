# Rogue Agents: release notes

## The fifth release (November 2026): three stages rebuilt, and most of the game beside them

**It cannot join games of the fourth release, nor the other way round** (the two say so): everyone playing
together needs the same one.

### What to try first

- **A run:** Play alone, then Begin (or Play with friends). A run begins at the River Station, the Mill and the
  Line, or the Weather Base and the Summit Station, as its seed falls. From the River Station one way on is the
  Command Bunker, and from the Bunker one way on is the Quay and the Dry Dock.
- **The River Station:** throw the lever in the sluice house, and the river drains to a road under both bridges.
- **The Command Bunker:** the board in the guardroom that runs the gun corridor's three guns, and the blast
  lobbies, whose doors open on a clock.
- **The Quay and the Dry Dock:** the crane's box, sent from place to place from the crane's cab.
- **The proving ground** (Play alone, then Proving ground): each new part by itself, station by station.
- **Agents,** on the main menu: twenty-four agents, and what each of them can do.

### What is a first guess

- Every number in the three difficulties, the cast, the boons and sacrifices and the new weapons is a first guess,
  kept in the game's data files to be tuned after play.
- Hardly anyone has played the three rebuilt stages, the squads, the cast or the new objectives yet. Bots have,
  by the thousand.
- Nobody has listened to a sound or watched a movement. Every new sound was made by the game's own tools and checked
  only by measurement (that no two of them could be taken for each other), and the guards' voices are stand-ins;
  the howl most of all is waiting for a person. Each movement was checked by its outline and in still
  pictures.
- What bots have measured: in the last whole runs written up (100 seeds a difficulty, played before the three
  stages were rebuilt), a bot that never misses, alone, won 100, 99 and 95 runs in 100 on Easy, Standard and Hard;
  a bot that misses, alone, won 100, 60 and 2; the same bot as four, 99, 99 and 90.
- A rebuilt stage is meant to take four people 8 to 11 minutes on Easy, 11 to 15 on Standard and 15 to 20 on Hard.
  A bot, which walks straight to each objective and looks round nowhere, is two to three times as quick, so how
  long a stage takes people is not known.
- The Docks' fullest view draws about 158,000 triangles, more than the stages were meant to; that was measured
  only on the development computer.
- Known and not yet explained: on a poor connection, someone who has joined another player's game may see a door
  or a hidden panel open and be pulled back as they walk into it. Step back and press Use again. (The game's own
  test players met something of the kind about one time in eight at one hidden room; a person has not been seen
  to.)

### The three rebuilt stages

- **Three stages are rebuilt,** each about four times the size it was: the River Station, the Command Bunker, and
  the Quay and the Dry Dock. Each has a thing of its own to work, a quiet way past the first fights, real glass,
  guns that are part of the place, an alarm bell that can be shot, rooms laid out differently from run to run, and
  two wings: shut on Easy, one of them open on Standard (the seed chooses), both on Hard. Each asks the new kinds of
  objective (below), and each has an ordnance locker, a curio case and places for a back office.
- **The River Station:** a logging camp and a research station on one river, from the boat landing past the camp
  and over the haul bridge to the mill yard, and on along the mine road to the mine. A giant tree stands over the
  yard with a deck round its trunk at eighteen metres, under a thatch roof, and hanging bridges leave from it.
  Trees, ferns and stands of growth fill the valley; the camp is built of logs and planks, and boards in mustard
  yellow name the landing, the tower, the mill, the sluice, the camp store and the mine. A careful team can go by
  the canal path, crouched under the bank, to the sluice house. There a lever shuts the gate in the weir: the river
  drains in twenty seconds to a dry bed you can walk, under both bridges, and a klaxon sounds for five seconds
  before the water comes back. On Standard and Hard the mill yard's guards come to open it again. Wade Shallows can
  cross on a gravel bar below the camp while the river runs. With the river drained, a charge can be planted on the
  log hoist's footing; the botanist, once found, is brought to her own boat at the laboratory's jetty. The yard,
  the camp and the log landing are laid out differently from run to run. Its wings are the station across the
  footbridge and the working side beyond the sluice.
- **The Command Bunker:** a command bunker under a hill on three levels. A gun corridor whose three automatic guns
  are run from a guardroom behind bulletproof glass: put them to sleep at its board, or, once, turn them on the
  guards for half a minute. Blast lobbies whose two doors open in turn on a clock, with a klaxon and a ring of lamps
  counting down; two levers in the commander's cabin hold every lobby's doors open, or shut, for a minute. An
  operations well whose east wall, two storeys high, is a lit plotting wall of cream panels with coasts and red and
  blue markers on it. A generator hall with a pit, and a lorry to leave in. A crouched cable way runs from the
  tunnel to the battery room, under it all. A yellow and black stripe on the floor leads from the way in to the way
  out, stencilled arrows point to the well, and every level has a light of its own. On Standard and Hard the guards
  put back a gun you put to sleep; on Hard, once the alarm has rung, one of them throws the lobbies shut. Its wings
  are the living quarters and the plant. The old Bunker is still a place to play against each other, as "The
  Bunker".
- **The Docks, rebuilt: "The Quay and the Dry Dock".** The team lands at the west end of a quay at night, crosses
  the walkway over a dry dock where a small ship stands on blocks nine metres down (the quiet way is along its
  floor, under her hull), passes a long transit shed with its customs office and locked bonded store, and comes to
  a crane over the foot of a freighter's gangway. The freighter can be walked from stern to bow: her house, bridge
  and boat deck, two holds and the engine room below, her forecastle and the boat that is the way out. Her crew
  have a gun on the forecastle and another on the boat deck. Behind a fence lie the container yard, its office block
  and its gate. **The crane is worked from its cab:** three buttons carry its box, in twelve seconds, to the apron
  (where it closes the way along the water), to hang between the quay and the ship (a second way aboard: with two
  or more players, someone must hold the button, or throw the catch on the ship's rail, while the others cross), or
  to the mouth of the yard (where it shuts the way the guards come from). Whoever stands where it comes down is
  moved aside. On Standard the crane's crew go up to put it back; on Hard they use it against you. Its wings are the
  container yard and the decks below.

### Fights and guards

- **The three difficulties are now Easy, Standard and Hard,** each with a line saying what it is, and they differ in
  kind, not only in how many guards there are. On **Easy** any shot to any head ends it, guards are slower off the
  mark, aim worse, hurt less, are further apart and keep well away from where you come in, one squad fights at a
  time, and a hit stops a guard for longer. On **Standard** a shot to the head kills at the range the gun is good
  at (beyond it, it only hurts badly). On **Hard** one ordinary guard in three wears a helmet that takes the first
  shot to the head; guards notice you sooner, aim better and hurt more; a colleague's gunfire carries further, so
  the neighbours come; and if the alarm's bell is still ringing half a minute after the alarm, three more guards
  come. Every one of these numbers is a first guess, kept in one file (`difficulty.json`) so that they can be
  tuned after play. Your own health, the guns and how guards see you are the same on all three. **The alarm on
  Easy brings three guards, not two;** on Hard more guards run for it (six times in ten).
- **Guards fight as squads.** Guards posted together share what they know: when one sees you, or hears your gun,
  they all know where you were. They scatter to cover of their own (a low wall, a crate, a pillar, a corner: there
  is cover wherever something would hide a guard, several hundred places a stage), get down, and come up one or
  two at a time to fire: there is always a moment, about a fifth of a second, when a head comes up before the
  shots. Only so many fire at one of you at once (two on Easy, three on Standard, four on Hard).
- **They work together, and they say so first.** One keeps your heads down while another runs to nearer cover:
  "Keep their heads down!", then "Moving!". The pinning fire is aimed high: stay low and it goes over you; stand
  up and it does not. When a fight has stuck, one of them may go round to come at you from another side, but
  never without the shout "Going round!" and a whole arm swept to the side he will take (in one fight in four on
  Easy, one in two on Standard, three in four on Hard). A guard who is badly hurt says so and runs for the next
  squad, or for the alarm; a squad never runs away all together. On Standard and Hard a squad that has you pinned
  in one place may throw a grenade: "Pineapple!" and an arm held high come first, and you have more than three and
  a half seconds from the shout.
- **Guards at doors and at glass.** One guard comes through a doorway at a time, a second and a half apart.
  Drop one in a doorway and for five seconds nobody follows: two stand either side of the frame with their backs
  to the wall, and one of the others may go round by another way, with the shout. Swing a door on hinges behind
  a guard who is not looking at it and he turns to see. A guard who sees you through a pane knows you are there,
  and shouts as he would. Ordinary glass they shoot through, at anyone they see behind it; resistant glass crazes
  white at their first rounds and they lose sight of you; bulletproof glass they rap with a gun, and come round
  for a clear shot.
- **Guards no longer run at you from across a room.** Only a heavy walks in. Lose them for ten seconds and someone
  says so, two come looking, and the rest hold their cover; twenty seconds and they stand down. A squad that only
  hears a fight comes if the fight has room for it, and otherwise waits at its own posts.
- **Guards talk, and signal with their arms.** Every line has an arm signal, so a fight can be read with the sound
  off: a fist up and punched forward for "Moving!", a flat hand patting down for "Keep their heads down!", an
  empty magazine held up, a rap on a pane of glass they cannot shoot through. Captions say what was said and from
  which side.
- **Fewer guns on you at once.** A bot alone is shot at about half as often as before, because only a few guards
  may fire at one player at once and the rest wait their turn. How hard that is for people is to be settled by
  playing.
- **Where a shot lands now decides what happens to a guard.** Eleven places, each with a reaction of its own that
  begins the moment the round lands: hit in the chest a guard staggers back with its arms windmilling; in the
  stomach it folds in half; in the knee it goes down on it and howls; in the foot it hops away on the other; from
  behind it is pitched forward and then spins round. A reaction lasts from half a second to a second and a third,
  by the place (a quarter longer on Easy, a fifth shorter on Hard), and while it lasts the guard can do nothing. On
  your own screen it begins as you fire, without waiting for whoever is hosting.
- **A gun can be shot out of a guard's hand.** It flies off to one side, and the guard is unarmed until it has
  scuttled over and picked it up. Take the gun first, and the guard throws up its hands and runs.
- **A round to the thigh leaves a guard limping, and one to the knee keeps it on that knee** for a few seconds.
- **Hats and helmets.** From Standard up, a round through a guard's hat takes the hat and nothing else. A heavy's
  helmet takes two rounds on Standard (the first knocks it over one eye, so you can keep count) and the third round
  to its head puts it down.
- **A guard's reload can be seen, and stopped:** the magazine out, a pat of the pockets, the magazine in, the gun
  up. A guard that is hit before the magazine is in has to begin again.
- **How a guard falls goes by where the last round landed and which way it came:** thrown back two metres by a
  round to the chest (four by a shotgun, a revolver or a blast), a slide on its face if it was shot from behind,
  and a fall of its own for each of the other places. A guard thrown at the water's edge goes in, and lies on the
  bed; one that is only staggered stays on the bank.
- **Guards run from a grenade** they hear land, after a moment's stare.
- **Other players' figures show when they are hit,** and a team-mate who is down is propped on one elbow and
  waving, so that nobody takes them for a body.

### What a stage asks

- **New kinds of thing to do,** asked in the three rebuilt stages: photograph something (hold your aim on it for
  two seconds), start a copy and hold the room while guards come for you, bring the power back station by station,
  carry a case out with only your pistol (the Docks and the Bunker), and stop a courier before they get away (the
  River Station and the Docks, on Standard and Hard). The Bunker's secondary objective is to throw two levers
  within ten seconds of each other (alone, twenty-five). One more kind, making three devices safe before their
  clock runs out, is built, but no stage asks it yet; the other eleven stages ask what they asked before. Lose one
  of them and it turns into something else to do, said on the screen.
- **In the rebuilt stages, on Standard and Hard, one line of the list is empty** until you find what it is about.
  Each rebuilt stage has a secondary objective (spare the staff, finish something before any alarm, turn the guns)
  that never stops you leaving; on Hard a second may be asked as well, as an ordinary objective.

### Agents

- **You choose who you play.** "Agents", on the main menu and in a room before a game begins, shows the cast one
  at a time: each agent's own figure, in a pose of their own; five things they are better or worse at than the
  standard agent (size, speed, health, armour, and how much their gun kicks); and what they can do. Four are there
  from the start: Agent Grey (the standard agent, as everyone has been until now), Mavis Stride, Big Morris Brisket
  and Dot Trunkcall. Only one of each in a game, whoever chooses first, except Agent Grey, of whom there can be any
  number.
- **They really differ, in a run and against each other.** A big agent is easier to hit and holds more; a small one
  is harder to hit (a shot at an ordinary head height goes over Thimble's) and holds less; guards aim at each
  one's own chest. Mavis can sprint for three seconds, though not shoot while she does. Big Morris charges four
  metres, knocking guards off their feet and bursting doors open (against another player it is a shove and a
  moment's wild aim, nothing more). Dozy Desmond slowly gets health back on his own, up to five. Dolly Skates is the
  fastest of all and takes a metre and a half to stop. The ability is on Q, or a controller's left stick click, and
  a small clock in the corner shows when it is ready again.
- **Twenty-four agents in all,** each with a look of their own and something they can do. Besides those above: Dot
  Trunkcall shows the team every guard near her through the walls; Lofty Frank reaches switches and fallen
  team-mates from twice as far; Dame Octavia Vibrato's high note breaks glass and rocks the guards near her;
  Topiary Tom, standing still, is a hedge guards are slow to notice; Mr Ledger's briefcase takes half of any hit
  from behind; Banger Bancroft is hurt little by his own explosives and thrown clear by them; Madame
  Maybe sees where a pickup will come back in a match; Stop-Sign Stan stops the guards in front of him dead, alarm
  runners too; Nana Ruckus's flask, once a stage, mends everyone round her; Norman the Kettle carries the most armour
  of anyone and whistles when he is badly hurt (and every guard near hears it); the Crushing Bore carries a fallen
  team-mate on his shoulder until they are back up; Parcel Pru throws further and carries more; Miss Shush runs
  without a sound and her shots carry less far; Wade Shallows wades where nobody else can (the River Station's
  gravel bar, the shallows at the Docks' landing steps, an overflow channel in the Bunker's tank room, and the
  proving ground's causeway); Brigadier Fuss is tough but jingles with every step; Mr Definitely-Human, once a
  stage, drops his coat instead of going down, and a stoat runs on; Inspector Trousers sees keys and hidden panels
  glint through walls; Skipper Limpet cannot be knocked about. Against other players each has a gentler version,
  and nobody's ability ever stops another player shooting or moving.
- **The other twenty are unlocked by a feat each, which the screen names** (Dozy Desmond, Thimble and Dolly Skates
  among them). Each agent has a line when chosen, when hit and when got back up, and a sound for what they do.

### Boons and the back office

- **Boons and sacrifices.** What you take at the end of a stage is now always a boon: something that costs nothing.
  You are shown three of different kinds, never one you hold or have been shown before in the run, and the first of
  the three leans to how you just played (quietly, with grenades, by getting people up, and so on). Thirty-one
  kinds of boon can be taken so far, among them Cold Trail, Duck Walk, Window Shopper, Domino, Hand-Me-Down, Tea
  Round, Second Opinion (draw your three again) and Rain Check (take nothing now, two next time). Five are for the
  new gadgets, and are offered only to someone carrying one: Cook's Hands (see where a throw will land, and hold a
  grenade for up to five seconds), Sticky Fingers (pick your own mines back up), Daisy Chain (your blasts set off
  barrels and mines twice as far away), Bank Shot (a throw that bounces first hits harder) and Pack Mule (carry two
  kinds of gadget). With Pebble you crouch and toss a stone, and a guard nearby walks over to look. Each run also
  leaves eight boons out altogether. Madame Maybe is shown four.
- **The back office.** Some stages of a run hide a small room behind a panel of wall: the run chooses which, two in
  most runs (on Hard, one run in four has a third). Eight stages have places for one: the River Station, the
  Command Bunker, the Quay and the Dry Dock, the Country House, the Picture Gallery, the Island Gaol, the Weather
  Base and the Hill Town; a run through the other six meets fewer. The proving ground always has one, behind the
  range hut's panel, to try. Nobody tells you where.
  Look for the lamp with red paper moths on it and a scuffed arc on the floor, listen for a ticking hum, and push the
  panel. Inside, three cases on a counter each hold something strong and say plainly, in red, what it costs. There
  are fifteen such sacrifices so far: the old prices (Glass Cannon, Heavy Kit, Lone Wolf, Hair Trigger) live here
  now, with eleven new ones such as Magnet, Travelling Light, Tunnel Vision, The Loud One, Debt and On the Clock.
  One of them, Finders Keepers, is there only when someone carries a curio: the curio you hold never runs out, and
  every curio case in the stages after is empty for your team-mates. Hold Use on a case to take it: one each, first
  come first served, and nobody can take while they are down. Some prices fall on the whole team; then the others
  see a banner while your hand is out, and it takes twice as long. Firing a gun in the room, or a guard raising the
  alarm nearby, brings a shutter down for twenty seconds. The vote's cards show a moth for a stage that hides a
  back office: always on Easy, and on the others only if you finished the stage's secondary objective.
- **A ping.** Press Z (or the middle mouse button, or click the right stick) to put a mark where you are looking, in
  your colour, for your team to see for three seconds. While a team-mate reaches for a sacrifice whose price you
  would pay, a ping says no: if most of the others say no too, they cannot take it.

### Explosives and odd weapons

- **The gadget is a fifth slot:** key 5 takes it in hand (fire throws, the second button sets a mine down in front
  of you), and G throws it without putting your gun away. On a controller: the left and right shoulder buttons. It
  holds six kinds, found in an **ordnance locker**: a green steel cabinet off the main way through, in every one of
  the fourteen stages. Press Use at its doors and it holds one kind for each of you, and from the second stage on,
  in some places, a grenade launcher or a rocket launcher (two for three or four players). The **Fuse mine** sticks
  where it lands and goes off four seconds later, ticking; the **Nosy mine** chirps and goes off at feet that are
  not creeping (creep past it at a crouch); the **Clicker mine** waits for your second button; the **Pineapple** is
  a grenade you can hold to shorten its fuse (hold the button and it stays in your hand, clock running), or roll
  underarm; the **Tripline mine** stretches a taut wire across a corridor that anyone crossing sets off, even a
  door swinging through it; the **Leapfrog mine** jumps to head height with a twang before it goes off, so ducking
  behind low cover does not help. Set any mine down without a sound with your second button. Only you and your
  team see the rings round your mines; everyone sees a Tripline's wire. Whoever shoots a mine owns its blast.
- **Things that fly, and things left lying.** The Terrier's round is now a real thing in the air: it is lobbed in
  an arc, takes a moment to arrive, and bursts where it comes down. One that hits something within four metres of
  the muzzle only bounces. Its second button lobs a bouncer, which goes off two and a half seconds after it leaves.
  The rocket flies straight under a trail of smoke, and its second button bursts it in the air. Glass and doors
  answer each explosive differently: a rocket takes a door off its hinges for good.
- **A blast you set off no longer hurts your team-mates in a run:** it shoves them. It still hurts you. In a
  match it hurts everyone but your own side.
- Any shot of yours sets off a mine or a round in the air, and a blast sets off the next one. (A guard's
  bullet sets off only what has landed.)
- The rocket launcher and the grenade go by working names (the Wolfhound, the Pineapple) that may yet change.
- **Four guns gain a second button.** The **Sparrowhawk** fans the rest of its magazine across the room; the
  **Buzzard** puts its legs down when you hold it on a wall-top or crouching, steadier but rooted; the **Lurcher 12**
  fires a single slug that reaches far and goes through a door; the **Airedale** walks three shots up a doorway.
- **The curio case.** Every stage has a glass-fronted case off the way through. A stage can have a second, which
  opens when you finish its extra objective; none does yet.
- **Eight odd weapons fill the cases.** Each rides beside one of your guns: press that gun's key again to change
  to it (with no such gun, the key goes straight to it). It goes with you from stage to stage with what is left in
  it, and the case of the next stage holds a different one where it can. Take a different one and yours stays in
  the case with what is left in it, for you or a friend to take back; and a friend now sees it in your hands. The
  **Otterhound** is a fire hose that pushes and never hurts: wash a guard off a ledge or into the water, short out a
  mounted gun, slide a fallen friend round a corner; fill it at any water. The **Heron** fires a steel bolt that pins
  a guard to the wall behind him, or a door shut where it stands, on a thin steel line: the second button winds it
  home and lets go. The **Bantam** is a wind-up tin hen: hold to wind her, let go, and she struts off and goes off
  at the first guard she reaches (never at you). The **Great Dane** is one enormous shot, and its kick throws you
  three metres backwards: across a gap, or off a roof. The **Jackdaw** fires a claw that brings back whatever it
  catches: rounds, a key, a mine, a hat, or the gun of a guard who is not looking. The **Woodpecker** nails a
  guard's boot to the floor, or his sleeve to the wall. The **Lyrebird** fires a box that plays a gunfight where it
  lands, which guards believe once. The **Bellbird** rings a note that breaks glass and sends guards' hands to their
  ears. In a match there is one in the arena, and whoever holds it drops it where they fall. Their shapes and
  sounds are first stand-ins.
- **In a match,** the host now chooses what lies about: everything, guns only, or curios and pistols ("one shot" is
  always guns only). Each player may have two mines down at once, and a fallen player's mines go out ten seconds
  later.
- Guards in each place now carry their own favourite found guns more often, so the stages of a run bring different
  guns round.
- **Two more guns have new names:** the rifle is the Goshawk (it was the Harrier) and the marksman's rifle is the
  Gannet (it was the Osprey). A search found a well-known game's rifle under each of the old names. Nothing about
  how they shoot has changed.

### Things in a stage

- **The proving ground** (Play alone, then Proving ground): a training camp outside the run with one of every new
  thing to try, station by station, each with a painted board that says what it is: glass of three strengths, a row
  of five kinds of door, a canal you let down with a lever and cross by its bed (or, as Wade Shallows, wade by its
  shallows), a gun gallery with a mounted gun to take, an automatic gun, a camera and the alarm's bell, a range with
  mines, a grenade and a launcher on a table (they come back after you take them), an ordnance locker and a row of
  cases holding all eight odd weapons, a squad of four to watch at work, and a room behind a panel. Sign the book
  at each station and walk out.
- **You can see through glass and water.** Glass comes in three strengths, made to be told apart at a glance:
  ordinary glass is clear, in a thin plain frame; resistant glass is green, in a thick frame, with wire in it;
  bulletproof glass is blue, in a steel frame with bolts. Seen from the side, a pane bends the view through it a
  little and shows the sky. The three rebuilt stages have real panes (the Bunker's guardroom looks onto its gun
  corridor through bulletproof glass), the show cases in the Gallery's east wing are real cases now, with things in
  them, and the proving ground has a glass house; in the other stages the windows are still pictures on walls.
- **Glass breaks.** Every pane in every stage now does what its kind (told by its frame and tint) says. Ordinary
  glass shatters at the first shot, loudly, and the round goes on through; resistant glass turns white and
  cloudy when hit (nobody can see through it after that) and gives way after a few more, or at once to the Magpie
  or the Gannet; bulletproof glass never breaks. A blast breaks every ordinary pane near it, and most break a
  resistant pane close by; a rocket breaks either kind anywhere in its reach. What is broken stays broken, and is
  the same for everyone in the game, including whoever joins later or takes over as host.
- **Water shows its bed in the shallows and loses it in the deep.** Its ripples bend what lies under it, and the
  sky lies on it: off the Yacht's stern the moon lays a path on the sea. The River Station's river, the Cave's lake
  and the sea round the Yacht, the Docks and the Lighthouse are all water of this kind. Only Wade Shallows wades,
  and only where a stage has shallows for him.
- **Nobody steps off the side of a bridge into the stream,** and at the water's edge you can stand in the
  shallows: deep water is walled off by the game itself now, to the same rule everywhere. In the Cave the
  lake's shore can be walked all the way round, which the guards there could not do before.
- **Doors on hinges.** Many doors (most of those in the Command Bunker and the Docks, and some elsewhere) now swing
  open when walked into or when Use is pressed at them, and shut themselves a couple of seconds later. A door never
  shuts on anyone: it stops and waits. Guards open them as they come; once the alarm has gone up, the doors they
  came through stay open. Doors on a clock (the Bunker's blast lobbies), hidden panels (the back office) and water
  that rises and falls (the River Station's sluice) are in a run now; the proving ground's door row also has a door
  worked by a lever, with a lamp over it.
- **Guns that are part of the place, and things that watch.** The three rebuilt stages have them: the River Station
  a mounted gun at the haul bridge's north end, another on the mast over the log landing and an automatic gun in
  the mine's roof; the Docks the freighter crew's two guns and automatic guns at the bonded store's door and the
  office block's; the Bunker its gun corridor's three and two mounted guns, over the generator pit and in the
  vehicle dock. Cameras so far are only in the proving ground. **An automatic gun** hangs from a ceiling or a wall
  and lights the floor it watches; when it sees you it whines for most of a second before it fires, and it turns
  slowly, so a run across its arc can be made. Its red box is its weak spot: two pistol rounds from anywhere put it
  out, and from outside its arc it cannot answer. A lever or a switch can put it to sleep, and the Bunker's board
  turns its guns on the guards for thirty seconds. **A mounted gun** behind a steel shield: press Use behind it to
  take the grips; it turns through a third of a circle, fires fast and grows hot (a gauge over your sight says how
  hot; too hot, it hisses and waits). Use again, or being knocked, lets go. Its shield covers the gunner's chest
  from the front; his back is open. Guards take one when you are in front of it, and leave it when you go round. **A
  camera** sweeps its arc with a pale light; let it hold you for a second and a half and the alarm goes up. A shot
  or two puts it out. **The alarm's bell** (in the rebuilt stages) can be shot: the ringing stops for everyone, and
  the alarm brings fewer guards, later. On Easy the guns sleep and nobody mans one until an alarm. In a match there
  are no cameras or bells; the guns are anybody's, and an automatic gun fires at everyone.

### The look and the screen

- **A new look, and the old one kept as a setting.** The picture is sharper (720 lines) and every surface of
  every stage has been redrawn: bigger, chunkier bricks and planks, textures twice as fine and smoothly filtered,
  each stage's concrete, wood and steel in that stage's own colours, and corners of rooms a little darker, so a
  room reads from its doorway. The old look is still there: Settings, Picture, "Look: Classic" (480 lines, or 240,
  small soft textures, no sky). Both show exactly the same things at the same distances; choose by taste or by
  how quick your computer is. The "Lines drawn" and "Soft textures" choices are now part of the look.
- **The night stages are lit by their own lamps**, each with a fitting you can see: the Cave's lamp posts now
  light the way (they had been lighting nothing), the Mansion has lamps over its galleries and a moon over its
  garden, the Docks are under sodium lamps on posts. **Guards and players take the light of where they stand**,
  in both looks: darker in the shadows, never so dark that they are lost.
- **A new sky,** in the new look, over every stage out of doors: a dome from the stage's colour overhead down into
  its haze, cloud that drifts (fast, in the Lighthouse's gale), a sun or a moon, stars at night, and the far skyline
  standing in the haze. There is dusk over the Forest, a gale and a moon over the Lighthouse, an overcast white over
  the Snow, a city's glow over the Office, the Gallery and the Docks, and first light over the Village and the
  Observatory. Where a stage ends, it still fades into its fog.
- **A new setting, "Glass and water"** (Settings, Picture): Full, as above, or Plain, which is quicker: tinted
  glass and water you can see through, with nothing bent and nothing reflected. On a slow computer try Plain.
- **People are people now, not stacks of boxes** (in the new look). Every guard, heavy, person and agent is one
  smooth figure with a face (eyes, brows, a nose, ears, a moustache or spectacles here and there), mitten hands,
  boots and a hat of their unit's kind: bush hats at the River Station, fur hats in the Snow, berets in the Bunker,
  sailor's caps on the Yacht, sou'westers at the Lighthouse, and so on, each stage's guards in its own colours. No
  two guards in one room look the same (six head shapes, four faces, a little taller or shorter, broader or
  slimmer). The heavy is a slab in a helmet and a vest of plates. Each agent looks like their card: Big Morris in
  his boater and striped apron, Desmond in his nightcap with his bear, Dolly on wheels. Your colour is on your
  shoulders, cuffs and hat band, and your mark (a bar, two bars, a chevron or a disc) on your back, so team-mates
  know each other from behind. Where a shot lands is judged exactly as before: the figures were made to the same shapes.
- **Every gun has its own model**, in your hands, in a guard's, lying on the floor and spinning as a pickup: the
  Wren's long silencer, the Magpie's fat cylinder, the Kestrel's row of holes, the Corncrake's fins, the Goshawk's
  hoop of a stock, the Airedale's drum, and so on, so you can tell from across a room what is lying there or what
  a guard is pointing at you. Your gun is held in gloved hands with your colour at the cuffs. Mines, grenades and
  rockets have their own shapes too.
- **Classic keeps its boxes:** in the old look people are still blocks (now with a face on the front of the head) and
  guns are the simple models, as befits it.
- **A new screen, in a typeface of the game's own.** Every word in the game is now drawn in one heavy, rounded
  lettering made for it, crisp at every size. The ammunition has moved from the corner into the crescent: an arc
  of rounds just inside it (a pip for each round, a bar for a big magazine, red when nearly empty), the count above
  it, your agent's mark beside it (which is also your ability's clock), and your gadgets as small diamonds. The
  objectives are a short list at the top left that sits back to a column of boxes and counters, and comes forward
  when something changes; big news (an objective done, failed or new, a team-mate down, the way out) is a banner
  at the top, one at a time, with smaller news in lines under it. Team-mates are small rings under the objectives,
  filled with their health, with their mark. Being hit shows a wedge on a ring at the edge of the view pointing
  the way the shot came from (solid when it hurt you, a hollow steel outline when your armour took it), and a tint
  at that edge. The screens between stages show the three offers and the vote for the way on as cards, with the
  clock running down along their foot, and who is ready by their marks. The menus are in the same lettering and
  shapes. Classic draws the same screen in its old pixel letters.
- **Three new settings** (Settings, Game): Steady view (being hit does not shake the view), Stronger outlines, and
  Hide the room code (six dots in its place during play, for anyone showing their screen to strangers).
- **Stages are called by their own names:** the River Station, the Mill and the Line, the Weather Base and the
  Summit Station, the Command Bunker, the Slate Mine and the Show Cave, the Motor Yacht, the Hill Town and its
  Harbour, the Country House, the Picture Gallery and Saleroom, the Island Gaol, the Tower, the Quay and the Dry
  Dock, the Causeway and the Light, and the Mountain Observatory (in a match too). The players' four colours are
  now the same on the screen and on their figures (cyan, magenta, lime and violet), each with its mark: a bar, two
  bars, an open caret or a hollow diamond. The rounds left in the magazine are drawn as brass rounds.

### Runs and playing together

- **A run can be put down between stages and carried on another day.** Your game writes the run down each time
  the next stage is settled. On the main menu, **Carry on** takes up a run you were playing alone, at the stage
  that was next, with everything you had: the same stage, the same guns, powerups and figures. Quit in the middle
  of a stage and you carry on from that stage's beginning (the pause menu says so before you leave). Beginning a
  new run alone asks first, because it takes the saved one's place.
- **Carrying on with friends:** under Play with friends, each run you put down with others has a row. Anyone who
  was in it can make a room (or host on this network) that carries it on; those who join see what it is before it
  starts, and whoever was in it gets their own place back as they join: their own game proves it was there, with a
  key that never leaves their computer, so nobody else can take their place. Whoever does not come is left out.
  Choose whether newcomers may join too: they start with the standard kit. If two of you each make a room for the
  same run, those are two separate games.
- Your record (Kit and records) counts a run once, when it is won or lost, however many times it was put down on
  the way. Runs are kept on your own computer only: the newest you played alone and the newest three with others.
  A saved run from another version of the game, or a damaged one, is set aside, and the main menu says so.
- **How games talk to each other was rebuilt,** to make room for larger stages, more guards, glass, doors, things
  that fly and a cast of agents. Each player is now sent what matters to them first: whatever they can see or are
  fighting in every update, the far side of the stage a few times a second.
- **Only what can be in the picture is drawn.** Every stage is cut into sectors that know which of the others
  they can see, and what is out of sight, out of view or lost in the fog is left out. The picture is the same to
  the pixel, and it is what lets stages be several times larger than they were.

### Fixed

- **In a very large fight, some shots and hits could go unseen and unheard** by anyone who had joined somebody
  else's game. Each is now played once for everybody.
- **A guard with no waypoint near it could set off for the wrong end of the stage,** and one sent on a very long
  way round finished it by walking in a straight line.
- **A player caught in the air against a wall, over a bank too steep to stand on, could hang there** and, much
  later, be thrown out of the stage. (A bot found it, in the Forest, once in nine hundred runs.)
- **The game cannot linger after you quit.** Once in some thousands of runs here, a game that had shut its window
  went on running in the background, waiting on something outside itself. Now, if closing down takes more than
  eight seconds, the game notes where it was stuck and ends anyway. Nothing is lost by it: everything is saved
  before closing begins.

  the other, was too like one classic's gauges; an independent review of the game's originality said so.)

- It plays with the third release: nothing about how games talk to each other has changed.

## The third release (November 2026): first fixes from play

Everything the second release had, with what its first long play-through found wrong put right. **It cannot join

- **Fixed: Esc did not hold the pause menu open.** On a keyboard the menu opened and shut on the same press.
- **The alarm is no longer the loudest thing in the game.** A fault made every looping sound play at full whatever

- **Guns are a touch louder beside everything else:** a sharper crack, and the music and background step back for

- **Water is in the way.** Nobody wades any more: the Jungle's stream is crossed by its two bridges and the stones

- **A hit staggers a guard for half as long again,** on every difficulty; and a guard a long way off runs straight

## The second release (November 2026)

The first release that anyone can download. One to four agents fight through a run of four stages, chosen along
the way from fourteen, taking a powerup with them from each to the next; or play against each other. Everything
in it (names, places, pictures, sounds, music) is the game's own.

**Read this first:** this is an early version. Every picture and sound is a stand-in made by a program. It has
been played at length by one person on one Windows PC, who enjoyed it, and by automatic players by the thousand;
what has not been tried by anyone is listed under "Not yet tried", and the list is long and honest.

### Getting it going
| System | Download | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Windows 10 or 11 | `RogueAgents-win-x64-...zip` | Unzip anywhere, open `RogueAgents.exe`. Windows may warn that it does not know the program: "More info", then "Run anyway". |
| Linux (64-bit) | `RogueAgents-linux-x64-...tar.gz` | Unpack, run `./RogueAgents`. Needs a graphics driver with Vulkan. |
| Mac, Apple chip (M1 or later) | `RogueAgents-osx-arm64-...tar.gz` | Unpack and follow `READ ME FIRST.txt` beside the app: the Mac will object the first time. Needs macOS 14 or later. |
| Mac, Intel | `RogueAgents-osx-x64-...tar.gz` | The same. |

Nothing else needs installing. **Everyone playing together needs this same release:** it cannot join games of
the first release, nor the other way round (the two say so when they try).

### Playing with friends
- **In the same house (same network):** one player chooses Play with friends, Host on this network. Their pause
  menu (Esc) shows the address the others type under Join on this network. The host's PC may ask to let the game
  through its firewall (it uses UDP port 27817).
- **Over the internet:** this needs a lobby server, the small program that hands out room codes and introduces
  the players' computers to each other. **There is no public lobby.** Whoever looks after your group's games runs
  one and gives you its address, which goes in Settings, Game, Lobby address, once. Then: Play with friends, Make
  a room; tell the others the six-letter code; they choose Join a room.

### What is new since the first release
- **Eleven more stages, and a run that is four stages long.** A run begins in the Jungle, the Forest (a railway
  through a pine wood at dusk, a relay station behind a fence) or the Snow (a weather station round a frozen
  lake); which, the run decides. Then the team votes between three ways on, from the Bunker, the Cave, the Yacht
  (three decks of a ship at anchor), the Village (a street, a square, a walled yard on a hill) and the Mansion (a
  country house of two storeys round a great hall); then again, from the Gallery (picture rooms round a sculpture
  court, a vault), the Prison (two yards, a cell block of two tiers), the Office (in by the roof, down two floors)
  and the Docks (a container yard, a warehouse, a freighter); and last between the Lighthouse (a rock in the sea
  in a gale, and a tower climbed room by room to the lamp) and the Observatory (a track up a mountain at first
  light to a great dish), where a run ends. The last two are provisional and may change.
- **Twelve guns.** Each of the four weapon slots now has three kinds of gun: the one everybody starts with, and two
  to find. A revolver and a machine pistol; a silenced submachine gun and a heavy one; a marksman's rifle and a
  light machine gun; an automatic shotgun and a grenade launcher. A gun of a kind you are not carrying is changed
  for by pressing Use beside it, and yours is left in its place. Which kind lies where is different every run.
  (The eight new guns borrow the sounds and shapes of the first four for now.)
- **Small rounds lose their force with distance.** The silenced pistol does half its damage by 28 metres.
- **Six more powerups** (fourteen in all), two of them with a price: the Lone Wolf, who is hurt less and cannot be
  revived, and the Hair Trigger, which fires faster from smaller magazines.
- **Two more kinds of objective.** *Bring someone out:* talk to them and they follow you, opening doors as they
  come, and the mission ends only when they are at the way out with the team. (If they are killed it becomes
  "search the body", and the alarm rings.) *Plant something:* hold Use for four seconds at a place marked with four
  gold corners; what was planted stays there, blinking. Every stage has one of each among the eight things it can
  ask for.
- **Playing against each other.** Whoever hosts (on one network, or by making a room) can choose "Against each
  other" instead of a run: everyone for themselves, two teams, or One Shot, where any hit kills. It is played on
  one of eight places (the Hall, the Bunker, the Yacht, the Gallery, the Mansion, the Prison, the Office, the
  Lighthouse) with nobody home: no guards, nothing asked for. Everyone starts with the pistol; guns, armour and
  medical kits lie where they lie in the stage and come back twenty seconds after they are taken. Three seconds
  after falling you are back, somewhere far from the others. First to the kills wins, or whoever is ahead when the
  time is up. Every number in it is a first guess.
- **Losing the host no longer loses the stage.** Over the internet, if the player whose computer is running the
  game drops out in the middle of a stage, one of the others takes over and the stage carries on from where it
  was: the same guards down, the same objectives done, everyone where they stood with what they had. The game
  stops for six to eight seconds while everyone finds the new host, and up to three seconds of play are lost.
  (Before, the stage began again.)
- **Music.** A piece behind the menus; in a stage, a calm piece with a tense layer that comes in over it when the
  guards are after you and goes again a few seconds after they lose you; a short piece when a mission is won or
  lost. Its loudness is its own (Settings, Sound). Like every other sound in the game it is a stand-in made by a
  program.
- **For whoever makes the real sounds:** a pack of your own can be chosen in Settings, Sound; the game makes an
  empty one for you with a list of every sound it asks for, and checks a pack for mistakes.
- **Something carries over between runs.** The game keeps a record of what you have done over all your runs, and
  ten feats (finish a stage, win a run, a hundred guards down...). Five of them each unlock a **kit**: a different
  way to begin a run (a padded jacket, a revolver or a machine pistol in place of the pistol, or one powerup
  already taken). Choose it under **Kit and records** on the main menu; what a run earned is said on the screen at
  its end. The record is yours alone, kept on your computer.
- **A run gets a little harder as it goes:** more of a stage's guards are on duty for every stage already come
  through.
- **A crash report now says what led up to the crash.** As you play, the game notes the main things that happen
  (a menu opened, a stage begun and what it was made from, a player joining, the host changing). A report ends
  with the last of them, after the error and where in the program it was. And if the game is taken down too
  suddenly to write anything (a graphics driver's fault, a freeze ended from Task Manager), the report is written
  the next time the game starts, from the log the last session left. See "If something goes wrong".
- **Fixed: some doors could not be walked through.** In the first release a player could not walk into the store
  or the barracks in the Jungle's yard: the body stopped at the doorstep. Fixed for every door.
- **Fixed: the floor of the Mansion's garage flickered** between concrete and the yard's gravel.
- Footsteps on snow have their own sound.
- Each download now carries the licences of the software it is built on (the folder `licenses`).

### Known limits
- All pictures and sounds are stand-ins made by the project's own tools: boxes for tents, drums for radios.
- Guards walk through players (they do not collide with them), and characters are not lit by the level's lights.
- The difficulties change numbers (objectives, guards, supplies), not how guards behave.
- If the host's computer is lost during the pause between stages, the team plays the finished stage again.
- No names over team-mates' heads.
- Music and sounds are read from WAV files only.
- The Windows and Mac programs are not signed with a paid certificate, so both systems warn the first time.
- On Windows a console window may flash for a moment when the game is opened with a double-click.

### Not yet tried
One person has played it, on one Windows PC. Nobody has yet reported on:
- **How hard it is, for people.** The automatic players win almost every run on the first difficulty, so it may
  be too easy; the second and third are harder.
- **The sound and the music.** They were checked by measurement, not by ear.
- **Playing against each other, a change of host in mid-stage, and the kits,** with people rather than automatic
  players.
- **A controller.** Its support is written and tested in code; none has been plugged in.
- **A Mac.** The Mac downloads are made and checked for shape only.
- **A slow PC.** The target is 60 pictures a second on a 2017 laptop with built-in graphics; the figures say it
  should manage easily, but none has run it.
- **Real home networks.** Internet play is tested with real programs on one PC, with the connection made poor on
  purpose, and Linux with Windows through a container; not between two houses.
- **A Linux PC with a graphics card.** The Linux build is checked in a container that draws in software.

### If something goes wrong
If the game stops unexpectedly it saves a report and says where: a file named `crash-...txt`, in a folder
`crashes` beside its settings (`%APPDATA%\RogueAgents` on Windows, `~/.config/RogueAgents` on Linux,
`~/Library/Application Support/RogueAgents` on a Mac). If it was taken down too suddenly to say anything, it
writes the report, and tells you so, the next time it starts. Sending that file to whoever gave you the game is
the most useful thing you can do.

A report holds what went wrong and where in the program, the last things that happened before it, what the game
was doing and what kind of computer it is. It does not hold your name or anyone else's, no internet addresses and
no room codes, and it is plain text: read it before you send it if you like. The folder `logs` beside it holds
the notes of the last five times the game was played, of the same kind. Nothing is sent anywhere by the game
itself.
